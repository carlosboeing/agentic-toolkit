# `/start-research`

Investigate a bounded question before, during, or independently of other lifecycle work. Research supplies evidence, not approval of product scope.

## Synopsis

```text
/start-research [question or source-path]
/start-research help
```

If the entire argument is `help`, `--help`, `-h`, `?`, or `usage`, or such a token follows a source path, show this synopsis and stop. The same words inside the question are content.

## Procedure

1. Read the question and named sources. Without a clear question, discover relevant project context and ask only for the consequential missing scope. Identify the decision the evidence supports, uncertainty, expected depth, and a stopping condition. Enough evidence for that decision is sufficient; report gaps instead of implying exhaustive coverage.
2. Discover relevant available skills, connectors, search, browser, experiment, and computer-use capabilities. Read applicable tool instructions. Use an explicitly requested specialist method; `deep-research` can supply a comprehensive multi-source method when its scope, facilities, and delegation are applicable. No background agent or parallel work is required. Delegate only within actual authorization and available facilities; otherwise work serially.
3. Use the least access sufficient. Prefer primary evidence such as official documentation, specifications, source code, or first-party data. Trace consequential claims to the owning source and distinguish observation, inference, and uncertainty. Verify facts that can change and record dates or revisions where useful. Interviews and experiments can supply evidence when their scope is authorized.
4. When no account is needed, prefer an isolated browser session. Access to the user's authenticated profile or desktop needs explicit authorization unless already granted for this scope. Posting, purchasing, installing, or a spike with writes or external effects needs its own bounded authorization. A read permission grants no blanket action permission. If a capability is missing, explain the evidence gap and request only the additional access needed.
5. Synthesize findings, alternatives, limitations, and implications for the original question. Save a report when requested or when decisions, review, or recovery warrant it; otherwise respond in chat. Cite sources beside claims and link to the work they inform. Do not create a mandatory research phase or document before design.

## Context, authority, and completion

Project instructions own location, metadata, review, and Git rules. Use the declared working-memory location and actual artifact types, including legacy layouts; do not create folders until needed. Read relevant prior lessons and applicable product and architecture sources. On resume, reconcile source revisions, uncommitted work, Git checkpoints, and current authorization rather than trust a recap or status alone.

A phase-only request stops at its boundary. Explicit full or partial autonomous scope can authorize included transitions and delegated reviews; record the scope, retained human gates, and stopping point once in an existing durable project record when recovery needs one. Personal human approval and readiness under delegated review are different. Use the project's review evidence and accountable reviewer convention; never claim the human read an artifact merely because an agent reviewed it. Commit, push, publication, installation, and other effects follow actual authorization and project rules.

For substantial handoffs, compare consequential obligations with their original authoritative sources as well as the preceding artifact. Retain stable references and account for preserved obligations, authorized changes, exclusions with reasons, and unresolved decisions. Inspect source revision differences and reconcile affected downstream work and reviews before proceeding. Keep scope, authority, verified progress, evidence, unresolved decisions, and next checkpoint visible through existing records. A small change can keep this context in session.

Capture reusable observations where encountered, with context, evidence, workaround and result, confirmed cause or uncertainty, and implications for later work. Use an existing record or a separate lesson when its discovery or concurrent writing value warrants it. Do not record every transient tool error as a permanent rule.

End with the result, material gaps, and a proportionate next-step recommendation. Explain whether the same session or a fresh session better fits context and review needs, and recommend independent or human review when consequence warrants it. A recommendation authorizes no later phase. Stop for retained human gates, the requested endpoint, or consequential decisions outside delegated scope.
